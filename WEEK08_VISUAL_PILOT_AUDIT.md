# Week 8 Visual Pilot Audit — Hybrid V2

## Goal

Make the Week 8 study guide substantially more visual without changing the lecture-defined examinable scope, while bringing the visual language of the actual lecture and Tutorial 5 into the public study guide.

## Visual system

The page now uses two complementary layers.

### Layer A — clean summary diagrams

1. **Week 8 conceptual roadmap** — metabolism → free energy → coupling → redox → enzymes → kinetics.
2. **ΔG decision map** — exergonic, equilibrium, endergonic.
3. **Pathway-driving map** — ATP coupling and product removal.
4. **Redox / NAD+–NADH map** — electron transfer from fuel through NADH.
5. **Activation-energy diagram** — catalyzed versus uncatalyzed pathways with the same overall ΔG.
6. **Michaelis–Menten saturation curve** — Vmax, half-Vmax, and Km.

### Layer B — course-source visual adaptations

7. **Tutorial 5 NAD/NADH “taxi” analogy** — preserves the unloaded/loaded carrier metaphor used in tutorial.
8. **Tutorial 5 enzyme catalytic cycle** — preserves the enzyme → ES → EP → enzyme sequence used in tutorial.
9. **Lecture 8 hexokinase induced-fit example** — preserves the lecture's open-versus-glucose-bound conformational-change logic.

The course-source adaptations are visually marked with a **COURSE VISUAL** label on the web page.

## Source integration

The visuals synthesize or faithfully adapt:

- Week 8 lecture slides, especially 8:7–8:10, 8:14–8:17, 8:19–8:28;
- Tutorial 5 review of Gibbs free energy, coupling, redox, enzyme specificity, activation energy, Km, and NAD+/NADH;
- Alberts et al. (2023), Chapters 3–4, for the underlying mechanistic explanation.

## Public-site visual policy

The site now follows a hybrid rule:

- preserve **lecture/tutorial visual metaphors and teaching logic** when those visuals are central to the way students encountered the material;
- redraw those course-source graphics into clean, web-native SVGs rather than publishing raw slide screenshots;
- continue to summarize textbook-derived concepts in original diagrams rather than reproduce textbook figures/pages verbatim.

This keeps the site visually connected to the course while maintaining a coherent web design and avoiding unnecessary redistribution of copyrighted textbook artwork.
