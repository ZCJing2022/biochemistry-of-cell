## M01

A reliable comparison uses the same criteria for both terms. Catabolism moves from larger starting molecules to smaller products; anabolism assembles larger molecules from smaller precursors. Building generally requires an energy input, while breakdown can supply both usable energy and intermediates for building. The distractors either reverse the directions, confuse metabolic reactions with measuring metabolites, or incorrectly separate pathways that can share materials. Whether the stem presents a definition, two cellular activities, or a student's mistaken labels, identify the direction of transformation first and then connect the energy requirement to that direction.

## M02

The correct description specifies both the objects and the measurement: identify small metabolites and quantify their amounts in a sample. Their levels can change after a meal or another physiological change, so sampling time and conditions are part of interpreting the result. Sequencing DNA and determining protein structures examine different biological information. Measuring one purified enzyme's maximum rate is also not the systematic metabolite profile described here. The decisive clue is therefore not merely that the investigation is biochemical, but that it measures the small-molecule metabolic state rather than genes, structures, or a single kinetic parameter.

## M03

Think of G as describing a free-energy state under specified conditions. A reaction question then compares two states by subtracting reactant G from product G to obtain ΔG. Neither quantity directly gives the number of molecules converted per second. That is a rate, while the concentration at half-maximal velocity is Km and enzyme number is an abundance measure. The wrong options are plausible biochemical measurements but answer different questions. Choosing the correct option requires preserving the distinction between usable energy, a change in usable energy, and the speed or capacity of the catalytic process.

## M04

Use the negative sign to make the conclusion that is actually supported: the products have lower free energy than the reactants in the written direction under the stated conditions. The remaining uncertainty is the activation barrier, which can still make conversion slow. Equilibrium would require ΔG = 0, and the enzyme does not have to change the endpoint energy difference to speed a reaction. The distractor about rapid completion makes a timing claim that no ΔG value alone establishes. Separate what the energetic evidence tells you from what would require a rate measurement or barrier information.

## M05

A standard reference condition is not automatically the condition in a living cell. Substrate supply and product consumption can change the actual reactant/product balance, so the same reaction can have different actual ΔG values in different cellular settings. The standard transformed value remains a reference value rather than changing simply because more enzyme is present. The distractors either confuse ΔG with an activation barrier or make the reference sign determine every possible cellular situation. Check which value is given and whether actual concentrations are known before drawing a conclusion about forward pathway flow.

## M06

Apply one rule consistently: negative ΔG is exergonic, positive ΔG is endergonic, and zero is equilibrium. Then check the explanation attached to equilibrium. The forward and reverse processes continue at equal rates, so no net concentration change occurs; they have not necessarily stopped and the reactants need not be exhausted. The distractors mix up signs or replace energetic categories with fast and slow. An exergonic reaction may still have a high activation barrier, so thermodynamic classification cannot be substituted for kinetics. The fully correct option gets both the signs and the dynamic meaning of equilibrium right.

## M07

## M08

Follow a catalytic cycle to distinguish enzyme from substrate. Substrate binds, is converted, and leaves as product; the enzyme remains available for further turnovers. Catalysis lowers the activation barrier and speeds the route toward the existing equilibrium. It does not supply a new product–reactant free-energy difference or make every uphill reaction favorable without coupling. The distractor about consuming enzyme therefore fails the reuse criterion, while those about changing equilibrium fail the thermodynamic criterion. The correct description includes specificity, repeated action, and the separation between changing rate and changing energetic favorability.

## M10

## M12

## M13

Reaction 2 is useful because of what it prepares, not because it immediately makes ATP. Phosphoglucose isomerase rearranges glucose-6-phosphate into fructose-6-phosphate, moving the carbonyl arrangement from C1 to C2. This supports the next phosphorylation at C1 and the later formation of two phosphorylated three-carbon units. The distractors wrongly assign direct ATP/CO2 production, acetyl-CoA formation, or phosphate removal to this step. Follow the sequence phosphorylation, rearrangement, phosphorylation, cleavage. That sequence connects the structural change to the pathway's subsequent carbon split and payoff reactions.

## M14

Together with reaction 1, this brings the investment to two ATP before cleavage. ## M15

## M16

Treat the fates as branches at pyruvate. The aerobic branch uses PDH to make acetyl-CoA for the CAC. The human muscle fermentation example reduces pyruvate to lactate to support NAD+ recycling. Alcoholic fermentation to ethanol and CO2 is the contrasting example in organisms such as yeast. These are not three consecutive steps followed by every pyruvate. The wrong options mix compartments, reverse the treated-as-irreversible PDH step, or assign ethanol production to the normal muscle route. Matching each fate to its biological context is as important as naming the end products.

## M17

There are two questions hidden in the comparison: how many electrons are represented by the reduced carrier, and how many can move in one transfer step. NADH transfers a pair. Fully reduced FADH2 also holds two electrons, but the flavin system can participate in one- or two-electron transfers. That flexibility connects pair-donating and single-electron carrier behavior. The alternatives incorrectly remove electrons from FADH2, require four-electron transfer, or substitute phosphate movement for redox transfer. Do not infer that FADH2-linked entry supports less pumping because its fully reduced form contains only one electron; entry route and transfer flexibility are different issues.

## M18

Follow the heme iron rather than the name cytochrome alone. Fe3+ accepts one electron to become Fe2+, and Fe2+ can donate one to return to Fe3+. This oxidation-state cycle accounts for one-electron transfer per step. It does not involve passing an entire NADH molecule, moving phosphate groups, or transporting only protons without redox change. The comparison with NADH matters because respiratory carriers need not all transfer the same number of electrons at once. The correct option gives both the single-electron count and the molecular component that changes state.

## M19

Follow the energy in order: favorable electron transfer, proton pumping, storage in an electrochemical gradient, and then ATP-synthase capture during proton return. The ETC's immediate energy-conserving task is pumping rather than direct phosphorylation of ADP. NADH is an electron donor, not a phosphate donor transferred directly to ADP, and CO2 concentration is not the intermediate energy store in this mechanism. This two-stage account explains why continued electron transport can coexist with poor ATP production when a membrane leak dissipates the gradient. The correct answer preserves the functional separation between the ETC and ATP synthase.

## M20

The chemical component arises from unequal H+ concentrations on the two sides of the inner membrane. The electrical component arises from separated charge and its associated membrane potential. ## M21

First label the sides of the inner membrane. Respiratory pumping moves protons from the matrix to the intermembrane space, and the pumping complexes identified are **I, III, and IV**. Complex II transfers electrons without pumping. During ATP synthesis, protons return in the opposite direction through ATP synthase, toward the matrix. The distractors either give every complex the same role, reverse the energy-building direction, or replace pumping with direct ADP phosphorylation. Keeping electron-flow arrows along the chain separate from proton arrows across the membrane prevents those errors.

## M22

Thermogenin/UCP1 is located where it can cross the respiratory proton barrier: the inner mitochondrial membrane of brown-adipose mitochondria. It supplies a route for proton return that does not drive ATP synthase. Consequently, less energy is captured as ATP and more is dissipated as heat. A soluble matrix enzyme or an outer-membrane oxygen-producing protein would not perform that function. Nor is thermogenin an outward pump that prevents proton return. The mechanism explains how fuel oxidation and oxygen use can continue while ATP yield relative to fuel decreases and thermogenesis increases.

## M23

Use the sequence **bind substrates, form product, release product**. Loose holds ADP and Pi, tight favors ATP formation, and open allows ATP release. Proton flow powers rotation of the internal rotor and γ shaft, and the stationary αβ catalytic units respond by changing conformation. They do not all spin together as a rigid wheel. The incorrect options import glycolysis, redox, or pumping tasks into these catalytic states. Distinguish release from the active site from later membrane export of ATP; the open state completes the catalytic cycle rather than acting as the nucleotide transporter.

## M25

## M32

The classification is **G-protein-coupled, enzyme-linked, and ion-channel-linked receptors**. Each describes a cell-surface arrangement that links extracellular recognition to an intracellular response. ## M34

Define the enzyme classes by their chemical operations. A kinase adds phosphate to a target protein, commonly using ATP; a phosphatase removes phosphate. Together they permit regulation of phosphorylation state in opposite directions. This does not mean that the kinase always activates and the phosphatase always inactivates: that depends on the effect of the phosphate on the particular target. GDP–GTP exchange belongs to a different switch mechanism, and seven membrane spans define GPCR structure rather than either enzyme class. The correct pairing remains valid regardless of whether the specific phosphate is activating or inhibitory.

## M35

Separate receptor architecture from G-protein organization. The GPCR is the seven-pass receptor, while its associated heterotrimeric G protein contains three subunits and a nucleotide-switching component. Ras is a monomeric small GTPase in the intracellular pathway. Both use the course's GTP-ON/GDP-OFF logic, but the shared chemistry does not make their assemblies identical. The distractors reverse the subunit arrangement, identify the receptor with Ras, or turn both switches into phosphorylation-only kinases. A complete comparison states both the common nucleotide rule and the different organization without collapsing separate proteins into one object.

## M36

## M37

Read RTK as describing the receptor's own catalytic capability. Its extracellular domain binds ligand, a single transmembrane segment crosses the membrane, and the cytosolic region contains intrinsic tyrosine-kinase activity. The ligand therefore does not need to be a kinase. A seven-pass GPCR has a different architecture and coupling arrangement, while Ras is a separate small GTPase and a phosphatase performs the opposite phosphate-removal operation. The correct option links outside recognition to inside enzymatic activity and avoids confusing the receptor with its ligand or with a downstream switch.

## M38

Activated RTKs use phosphotyrosine-containing sites to recruit intracellular signaling proteins. Those docking interactions assemble the machinery that relays the signal; Grb2 supplies the adaptor link emphasized before Sos and Ras in this pathway. The sites do not directly convert GDP into GTP, act as an electron sink, or store glucose. Their information lies in the binding opportunities created by phosphorylation. This is why receptor phosphorylation has a functional consequence beyond marking the receptor as active: it determines which downstream partners can join the signaling complex and help propagate the response.
